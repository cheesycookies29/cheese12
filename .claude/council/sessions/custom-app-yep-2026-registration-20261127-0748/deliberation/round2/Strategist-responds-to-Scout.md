## Strategist Response to Scout

**Their position:** Scout, citing pretix quotas, and Craftsman's draft invariants count capacity as confirmed + reserved + held seats (at most 10). Both treat reservations and short-lived holds as seat states that the transfer, swap and assign handlers must all count, and that the invariant and fuzz suite must cover.

**My response:** Modify

**Reasoning:** The council is arguing about two different things under one word. A **reservation** is mandatory: spec s.8 counts "active reservations" toward 10 even before arrival, s.10 says preferred no-show seats stay reserved with "no automatic expiry" until manual release, and acceptance scenario 1 tests exactly that. So `reserved` is a real state, and my round 1 Must #7 already counts it. Scout and Craftsman are right that it has to be in the invariant and fuzz suite. That means reserved-to-confirmed at check-in, release by an actor only, transfer and swap of a reserved guest, and a substitute taking over a reservation without a double claim. A **short-lived hold** is different. The spec marks it optional twice ("Temporary holds, if implemented", "Optional short-lived seat holds may reduce conflicts"), and it stays Won't. A synchronous check-then-`db.save()` handler leaves no window to protect. Delegation already gives each table one owner, so the only real contention is the Super Administrator against an owner, or one staffer on two devices. A hold would also make the arrival peak worse: a pick parked while a staffer talks to a guest blocks the last seat for everyone else. Holds would cost about 1.5 to 2 days (TTL, an expiry sweep, a fake clock in tests, release when a device walks away, ghost holds after a crash) to fix conflicts that a fast refusal with a reason code (`SEAT_TAKEN`, then refresh) already handles. Craftsman's real worry is that "handlers will forget to count them". I answer that with structure, not more states. Every seat claim is one record on the guest, `seat: {tableId, seatNo, status: 'reserved'|'confirmed'}`. One function, `claimsAt(tableId)`, counts every active claim **whatever its status**, and every handler (assign, transfer, swap, substitute, reclassify, table removal) must call it. Then no handler can forget a status, because none of them counts by status. The invariant becomes `claimsAt(t) <= capacity` for every table. The fuzz suite drives random sequences of assign, check-in, release, transfer, swap and substitute over seeded reserved and confirmed guests (about half a day more than Craftsman's draft). It also asserts that the status set is closed, so a `held` state cannot creep in untested. If a later client app truly needs holds, they become one more claim status with an `expiresAt`. The status-agnostic count covers them on day one, so leaving holds out now costs nothing later. Net: `reserved` is In, with full invariant and fuzz coverage. `held` is Out, and the claim model and closed-enum test leave room to add it later.
