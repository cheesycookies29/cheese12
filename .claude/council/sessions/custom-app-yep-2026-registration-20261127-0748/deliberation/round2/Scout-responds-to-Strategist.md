## Scout Response to Strategist

**Their position:** Short-lived seat holds are "Won't" (backlog #35) because one synchronous `commit()` leaves no time between the check and `db.save()`. Capacity still counts confirmed + reserved (backlog #7), and a preferred guest's pre-assigned seat stays blocked until someone releases it by hand (#6).

**My response:** Modify. I concede the `held` state. I keep `reserved` as a required, durable seat state, and the invariant and fuzz suite must cover it.

**Reasoning:** My Round 1 merged two things the spec keeps apart, so I correct it here. Section 8 makes **reservations** mandatory: "Count all confirmed assignments and active reservations toward the 10-person limit". Section 21 and the "Preferred guest / no-show" acceptance scenario say what a reservation is: a preferred guest's pre-assigned table and seat that "stays blocked until manual release". **Holds** are optional in the spec: "Temporary holds, *if implemented*" (s.8) and "*Optional* short-lived seat holds may reduce conflicts" (s.16). The pretix precedent I cited supports Strategist's cut. pretix counts unexpired cart positions toward a quota only because anonymous buyers spend minutes across many requests between choosing a seat and paying ([pretix quotas](https://docs.pretix.eu/dev/api/resources/quotas.html)). Seats.io's `holdToken` exists for the same reason: consumer checkout. Here a staffer chooses a table and confirms it in a single request, and the group-arrival case also assigns all or none in a single `commit()`. That leaves no checkout window to protect. A staffer's "pending pick" while talking to a guest can lose the race safely through pretix's other pattern, which I keep: a refusal with a reason (`409 {reason:"table_full", revision}`) and a refreshed list of eligible tables, as section 16 asks. A TTL hold would also add the only write that no request triggers (an expiry sweeper). That write would need its own audit record and restart recovery on the JSON DB, and it would bring back the time window that the synchronous commit removes. So the state machine is `seat.status ∈ {reserved, confirmed}` plus a released record in history, with **no `held`**.

Craftsman's worry that swap or transfer handlers will forget to count reserved seats is real, but the fix is structural, not a hold state. All capacity checks should go through one function, `claimsOn(event, tableId)`, that counts guests whose `seat.status` is `reserved` or `confirmed`. The fuzz suite should generate preferred-guest reservations in its random operations: assign, transfer, swap, release, unlisted approval, substitution, group arrival. After every step it checks Craftsman's invariant 1 (with `activeHolds` = 0) and invariant 10 (a reservation is released only by an actor, never by expiry). One more test makes adding holds later safe: an exhaustiveness test that fails when `SEAT_STATUSES` holds a value that `claimsOn` does not classify as counting or not counting. If the client asks for holds after 5 Dec, the test forces whoever adds `held` to decide how it counts.

**Net effect on the design:**
- Keep `reserved` (Must): it is part of backlog #6 and #7, it is created at import or by an administrator before the event, and it leaves only by `release` (with actor and reason) or by becoming `confirmed` at check-in.
- Drop `held` / TTL holds (Won't, as Strategist proposed). Last-seat conflicts are handled by a refusal with a reason plus a refresh.
- Invariant and fuzz: count `reserved`, assert that holds are 0, assert that no reservation is ever released by expiry, and add the status-exhaustiveness test.
