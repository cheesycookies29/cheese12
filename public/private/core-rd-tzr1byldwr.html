The name

Why Legati

lēgātus Latin, plural lēgātī

An envoy or commander entrusted to act with the authority of Rome, and held to account for how it was used.

A legatus carried real authority and answered for it. That balance of power and responsibility is the standard we hold ourselves to.

Our mark is drawn from the meander, the continuous line that ran along Roman pillars and temples. Its corners turn back on themselves and never break, a figure of strength and of stability. Inside it sit the letters L and G.

The pillar is a good picture of a holding company. It carries weight that is not its own, it is built to stand for generations, and it only works if it is sound all the way through.

Principles

Four pillars, one for each color of the house

  • Legati Green

    Trust

    We keep our word to the people and companies who depend on us. Trust is earned slowly and kept on purpose.

  • Legati Gold

    Excellence

    We set a high standard for the work of every business we hold, and we invest so that it can be met.

  • Legati Parchment

    Resilience

    We build for good years and hard ones: sound finances, steady leadership and decisions made for the long run.

  • Legati White

    Clarity

    We say clearly what we own, how we decide and what we expect, so that no one has to guess.

Approach

How we hold a business

For the long term

We build to keep. Our plans have no exit date, so decisions are weighed against the next ten years, not the next quarter.

The group sets standards, the companies run

Each company keeps its own leadership, name and customers. The group sets the standards, supports the people who lead, and holds them to account.

Responsible for what we hold

We expect to be judged on how our companies are run and on what they deliver, and we hold ourselves to the standards we set for them.
