## Before you start

**ATLAS Documents** is the school's file store: your own files, the folders each office or college shares, and the documents people share with you. Everyone who works at the school and every student uses it; parents and guardians do not.

To open it, sign in on the ATLAS home page and choose **Documents** under **Your systems** (or the grid button, **All systems**, from any other system).

What you can do depends on who you are:

- **Employees** (teachers, registrars, deans, HR, finance and other staff) keep their own files, work in the school folders shared with them, share documents with people and groups, and send links to people outside the school.
- **Students** keep their own files, share them with their teachers and classmates by name, and open what is shared with them. Students do not make links for people outside the school, and do not share with whole groups.
- **Administrators** (and anyone given **Document control** under **Roles & permissions**) also manage every document and folder, set the rules in **Document control**, and place legal holds.

Every file is checked when it arrives: ATLAS reads the file itself to tell what it is, whatever it is called. Programs, scripts, web pages, SVG drawings, Office files with macros and password-protected Office files are never accepted. Every file is kept encrypted on the server.

## How to…

### Add files

1. Open the folder: **My files**, or a folder under **School folders** where you can add files.
2. Choose **Upload** and pick the files, or drop them on the page.
3. Wait for each line under **Uploads** to say *Added as …*.

Files go up in pieces of 4 MB, so a large file survives a short network drop. The largest file allowed is shown under the list (50 MB unless your school changed it). If a file says *Not added*, the reason tells you what to do, for example *The file is called .jpg but it is a PDF file* (upload it with its real name) or *.exe files are never accepted*.

### Make a folder

1. Open **My files**, or a school folder where you can add files.
2. Choose **New folder**, type its name and choose **Add folder**.

Folders at the top of **School folders** are made by document managers (choose **New school folder**). Anyone who can add files to a school folder can make folders inside it.

### Find a document

1. Type words in the search box at the top of any list and press `Enter`.
2. Open a result.

ATLAS looks in titles, file names, document numbers (*DOC-2026-0001*), tags, descriptions, and the words inside text, CSV, e-mail, Word, Excel, PowerPoint and OpenDocument files. It does not read the text of PDFs and pictures: give them a clear title and tags.

### Open or download a document

1. Open the document from a list.
2. PDFs and pictures show on the page. For other files, choose **Download**.

To get an older version, open **Versions** and choose the download button on its row.

### Add a new version

1. Open the document and choose **New version**.
2. Pick the file. It must be the same kind of file as the document (a Word file for a Word document).

The new version becomes the current one; the earlier ones stay under **Versions**. To go back, choose **Restore** on an older version: it is added again as the newest version, and nothing is lost.

### Check out a document while you edit it

1. Open the document and choose **Check out**. Others can still open it, but only you can add a new version.
2. When you add your new version, the check-out ends by itself. Or choose **Check in** to end it without a new version.

A check-out also ends by itself after 72 hours (or what your school set). The person who manages the document can check it in for someone else.

### Share with people in the school

1. Open the document (or the folder) and choose **Share**.
2. Under **Add a person**, type at least two letters of a name and choose the person. Staff can also add a whole account type (**Or a whole account type**), a department (**Or a department**) or **Everyone signed in**.
3. For each one, choose **Can view**, **Can edit** or **Can manage**. Untick **Download** to let someone view a document without downloading it. Tick **Share on** to let them share it further.
4. Choose **Save access**.

You can never give more access than you have. Sharing a folder shares everything in it, and the folders inside it. Departments come from the employee records in ATLAS People.

| Level | What it allows |
|---|---|
| Can view | Open, preview and download (unless Download is unticked) |
| Can edit | Also add new versions, change the details, check out |
| Can manage | Also share, make links, move it and delete it |

### Send a link to someone outside the school

1. Open the document or folder and choose **Share**.
2. Under **Links for people outside the school**, choose **Make a link**.
3. Pick what the link allows: **View only** or **View and download** (and, for a folder, **Upload only**). Set how many days it works, a password, the most downloads, and a note.
4. Choose **Make link**, then **Copy**, and send the link. Send the password another way (by text or phone).

> **Important:** The link is shown only once. ATLAS keeps a fingerprint of it, not the link itself, so it cannot show it again: if you lose it, turn it off and make a new one.

The person opens the link without an ATLAS account. With a password, they type it first. Confidential documents always need a password; restricted documents are never shared by link. A link stops working when it expires, when its downloads are used up, when you turn it off, when you lose access to the document, or when the document goes to the trash. You are told on **My files** the first time someone opens it.

### Collect files with an upload link

1. Open a folder you can add files to and choose **Share**.
2. Under **Make a link**, pick **Upload only**, add a note (for example *Send your Form 138 and birth certificate*) and choose **Make link**.
3. Send the link. The person types their name and picks their files.

Their files arrive in the folder marked *Received, not reviewed*, with every check an upload from inside gets. They also appear under **Received**, and you are told on **My files**.

### Review received files

1. Choose **Received** in the sidebar.
2. Open each file and look at it.
3. Choose **Mark reviewed**.

### Turn a link off

1. Choose **My links** in the sidebar (or open the document and choose **Share**).
2. Choose **Turn off** on the link and confirm. It stops working at once.

### Delete or restore a document

1. Open the document and choose the bin button (**Move to trash**), then confirm. For a folder, open it, choose **Folder**, then **Move to trash**.
2. To bring it back, choose **Trash** in the sidebar and **Restore** on its row.

Items stay in the trash for 30 days (or what your school set), then are deleted for good. **Delete for good** in **Trash** does it at once. A document or folder on legal hold cannot be deleted.

### Change a document's details

1. Open the document and choose **Edit details**.
2. Change the title, description, tags or status (**Draft**, **Final**, **Obsolete**). If you manage it, you can also change its classification and move it to another folder.
3. Choose **Save**.

| Classification | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Public | Can be given to anyone |
| Internal | For people in the school (the usual) |
| Confidential | For the people it is shared with; links need a password |
| Restricted | Named people only; never shared by link; only those who manage it download it |

### For students: keep your files and hand one to a teacher

1. Open **Documents** and choose **Upload** on **My files**.
2. Open the file and choose **Share**.
3. Under **Add a person**, type your teacher's name, choose them, and choose **Save access**.

![A student's Shared with me, on a phone](documents-student.jpg)

What teachers share with you is under **Shared with me**; what the school shares with everyone is under **School folders**.

## Screens at a glance

| Screen | What it is for |
|---|---|
| My files | Your own files and folders; only you see them until you share one |
| School folders | The folders the school shares (each office or college), and folders shared with you |
| Shared with me | Documents other people shared with you |
| Recent | The documents you can open, newest changes first |
| Received | Files sent through your upload links, waiting to be reviewed (employees) |
| My links | The links you made for people outside the school; turn them off here (employees) |
| Trash | What you deleted, until it is removed for good |
| Document control | The rules for the whole school (document managers; see [the administrator's guide](admin#set-the-rules-for-documents)) |

## Questions

**ATLAS says "Not added: … files are never accepted".** Programs, scripts, web pages, SVG drawings and Office files with macros are never accepted, whatever they are called, because they can run code on a computer or in a browser. Save the document as PDF, or as a Word, Excel or PowerPoint file without macros (.docx, .xlsx, .pptx), and upload that.

**ATLAS says the file "is called .pdf but it is" something else.** ATLAS reads the file to tell what it really is. The name must match: rename the file with its real extension, or check that it is the right file.

**ATLAS says a kind of file "is not accepted here".** Your school has not switched that kind of file on (ZIP archives, audio, video and drawings are off at first). Ask an administrator.

**I cannot find a document someone shared with me.** Look under **Shared with me**, or search for its title. If it is still not there, the person may have shared it with someone else, or taken your access away; ask them.

**Why can't I share this document?** You need **Can manage** to change who has access, and **Share on** (or manage) to make links. Ask the person who manages it, shown as its owner.

**Why can't I download a document I can see?** It was shared with you as view only, or it is *Restricted*: only the people who manage a restricted document download it.

**Can students send links outside the school?** No. Students share with named people (teachers, and classmates in their sections), and only inside ATLAS.

**Is my file safe on the server?** Every file is encrypted with its own key, and its fingerprint (SHA-256) is checked each time it is opened. Only the people it is shared with can open it; to anyone else ATLAS says *That document or folder is not there, or you do not have access to it.*

**Someone says my link "is not available".** The link expired, its downloads are used up, it was turned off, the document went to the trash, or you no longer have access to it. Make a new one with **Share**.

**How much can I keep?** Each person may keep 2 GB of documents (all versions counted) unless your school changed it. **My files** shows how much you use.
