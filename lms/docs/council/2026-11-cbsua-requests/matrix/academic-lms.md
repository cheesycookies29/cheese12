# CBSUA end-user requests: Learn (LMS), Academic (UIS) and cross-system, mapped against ATLAS

**Date:** 2026-11-25 · **Source:** the CBSUA action-items punchlist from the October 15, 2026 executive meeting
(LMS tab PL-01 to PL-21, UIS tab PL-01 to PL-60, Cross-System tab PL-01). **Status:** analysis only; nothing is built.

These items are end users' feedback on the demos of the systems CBSUA uses today, from another vendor. They are
not about ATLAS. Each row below says what **ATLAS Learn**, **ATLAS Academic** and the shared platform do today,
read from the code (`lms/server`, `lms/shared`, `lms/public/js`) and the guides (`lms/docs/guides`), and what
would meet the request. The aim is to meet them through **configuration the offices own**, not through one
school's choices written into the code.

Statuses:
- **Has**: works as asked.
- **Configurable**: an administrator or the registrar can already set it.
- **Partly**: some of it exists.
- **Missing**: nothing yet.
- **Not ATLAS**: about the other vendor's own bug, template or rollout. The row still says whether ATLAS gets it right.

Effort: **S** = days, **M** = one to three weeks, **L** = more than three weeks (one developer, tests and guides included).

Two conditions from the earlier council ([../../2026-11-cbsua-modules/README.md](../../2026-11-cbsua-modules/README.md))
bound everything here:
- **Learn is "go with conditions" and Academic is "not yet".** The storage move to `node:sqlite` comes first, and
  `academicRecords` must be in a real table before any UIS import (UIS-PL-35, X-PL-01).
- **Grades need separation of duties.** Different people approve and post grades, and administrators lose
  transactional powers. Every approval route proposed below keeps that rule.

## Configurability themes

ATLAS already has a **configuration center**:
- **The Settings center.** `shared/settings-catalog.js` lists every setting with its area, label and screen. `server/config.js` compares each with its default, keeps a change history with who changed what and the values before and after, undoes a change, restores defaults, and exports or imports the whole configuration as a file with a preview. Saves go through each module's own route and checks.
- **Learn's settings:** passing grade, enrollment rules, uploads, the quiz grace period, absence alerts, activity types and weights, and modules per account type.
- **Academic's settings:** the grading scale with checks, the lowest passing grade, INC days, dean's-list rules, the registrar's name and title, document fees, and admission requirements by student type.
- **Numbering:** student, employee and document-request numbers, with `{YYYY}`, `{YY}`, `{MM}` and `{N}` tokens.
- **Roles & permissions:** add or remove permissions on the built-in account types, plus per-account access.
- **Branding:** school name, theme colour, one logo, the sign-in headline; campuses with name, code and address.
- **Keycloak SSO**, with "Set up for me".

What is **not** configurable today:
- document layouts;
- the remarks codes;
- the grade formula;
- approval routes;
- report columns;
- imports.

The themes below group the items by the mechanism that would serve them. They are ordered by how many items each serves.

## Questions for CBSUA

These need a sample, a decision or a specification before building.

1. **TOR formats.** A sample of each variant in use: regular, transferee, graduate school, shiftee, old or legacy record, and for an undergraduate who did not finish. For each:
   - the exact header lines per campus (address, email, phone);
   - which logos and where (Bagong Pilipinas, ISO, the university seal);
   - watermark or none;
   - the "Doc. stamp paid" line;
   - the wording of graduation remarks and of "remarks during defect" (UIS-PL-02, 15, 16, 18, 21, 24, 53, 60).
2. **Certification samples.** GWA certification (per term or cumulative?), honorable dismissal, transfer credential, Certificate of Grades and of Enrollment for a chosen term, and the "additional certifications" each campus issues, with signatories and fees (UIS-PL-31, 32, 34, 42, 56).
3. **Form 137 for the laboratory high school.** A sample, and a decision: does ATLAS keep the laboratory high school's records (DepEd grading, quarters), or only print Form 137 from records kept elsewhere (UIS-PL-33, 44)?
4. **The remarks list.** Each code with its meaning and effect: AC, OD, UD, NA, NG, NRG, and whether NPE and S are retired. For each, say whether it passes, earns units and counts in the GWA, and whether old records using retired codes keep them (UIS-PL-14, 23).
5. **Grade formula.** The grading periods (midterm and final) and their weights, the transmutation table (for example base 50 or 60), rounding, and whether these differ by college. Also: may teachers set their own activity types and weights, and within what limits (LMS-PL-10, 13, 19)?
6. **Student numbers.** The code for each campus (SID, PIL, CAL, PHS, PAS…). Does the serial restart each year per campus? Are existing numbers kept as they are (UIS-PL-59)?
7. **Fees and FHE.** The fee matrix by program and year level, the RA 10931 eligibility rules CBSUA applies, a sample UniFAST billing (Form 2 with amounts), and a sample of the **UniFAST scholarship validation** file or process (UIS-PL-11, 26, 28, 39).
8. **Order of payment.** UIS-PL-38 and 51 read "no printing … no need for printing". Is an order of payment wanted on screen only, printed, or not at all?
9. **FTEES.** What it is, who runs it, and how it takes data (API, file or manual). Which data it needs (faculty, sections, students per section) and when (LMS-PL-15).
10. **The VLP export.** A sample export from the old LMS. Is it Moodle-based (which version)? Which content must move: lessons, files, quizzes, past grades (LMS-PL-06)?
11. **HRIS source.** Is ATLAS People the source of faculty records, or another HRIS? Which identifier links a faculty member across systems (LMS-PL-01)?
12. **Report definitions.** The masterlist columns, and what **FTE** and **REV** mean and how each is computed. The CHED enrollment list version in use (UIS-PL-25, 40).
13. **Holds.** Which requirements lock a student's grades, which office places and lifts each hold, and the policy behind it (UIS-PL-52).
14. **Approval routes.** The levels for post-lock grade changes (department head → VPAA → ?), who stands at each level per campus, and the grade cut-off dates per term (UIS-PL-09, 10).
15. **Exams.** Which exams need integrity controls, and on which devices: school laboratories or students' own phones (LMS-PL-14)?
16. **Meaning to confirm:** UIS-PL-47 ("status should not be editable"), UIS-PL-55 ("complete, not data by curriculum"), UIS-PL-35 ("old record"), and the pass marks in LMS-PL-05 (per quiz or per course?).
17. **Samples of what teachers use today:** rubric formats (LMS-PL-16), and the question file formats they would import, such as Word, Excel or Aiken (LMS-PL-18).

## Requests that conflict with ATLAS's design

| # | Request | The conflict | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Faculty accounts and roles managed in the LMS, apart from the UIS** (LMS-PL-07, 09) | ATLAS has **one account per person** across Learn, Academic, People and Finance, signed in through Keycloak. A separate LMS account would bring two passwords, two lists to keep current, and accounts left behind when faculty leave. | **Keep one account.** The request is already met in spirit: ATLAS's Admin panel *is* Learn's admin, and Academic has no accounts of its own. Make **People (HR) the source** for faculty, so the account is created with the employee record (LMS-PL-01). Roles stay in the one Roles & permissions editor. Refuse a second account store. |
| 2 | **Lockdown browser** (LMS-PL-14) | A web page cannot stop a phone or computer from opening other applications. Promising it would be false assurance, and a kiosk browser on students' own phones is not realistic. | **Do not claim lockdown.** Offer integrity options and say what each does: page-leave events logged and shown to the teacher, full-screen request, one question at a time, shuffled questions and choices, question pools, an access code, limits to the school's networks. For high-stakes exams in school laboratories, accept only **Safe Exam Browser** later, as an opt-in per exam. |
| 3 | **Teachers define their own exam and activity types** (LMS-PL-10) | Weights are university grading policy: they must be the same across sections of a course, and they are what a dean approves. Free types per teacher break comparability. | **Make it a policy setting, not a free-for-all:** school-wide types, or class types within limits (required types such as the exam, a share range, a 100% total). Show the weights on the grade sheet the dean approves. |
| 4 | **Super-administrator in the grade approval route** (UIS-PL-10, 12) | The earlier council made separation of duties a condition for Academic: different people approve and post grades, and administrators lose transactional powers. An ICT super-administrator who approves grade changes undoes both. | A **configurable route** of academic roles (for example department head → VPAA), each step a different person. The administrator configures the route but is never a step in it. "Override logs" are met by the audit log and the Settings center history. |
| 5 | **Auto-approval after some days** (this appears in the FMIS tab, for example "auto-approve after 3–5 days"; the same could be asked of grade sheets and grade changes) | An approval that happens by itself is not an approval. For grades it would post changes nobody checked. | **No auto-approval in Academic.** Instead, a reminder to the approver, then escalation to the next level or to a named alternate after N days (configurable), all logged. |
| 6 | **Manual grade overrides** (LMS-PL-13) | Overrides are needed (INC, dropped, rounding), but a final grade that silently differs from the computed one hides errors and tampering. | Allow the override **with a remark from the list and a reason**, kept in the enrollment history and shown on the grade sheet. After posting, a change only through the approval route. |
| 7 | **Locking grade viewing for unmet requirements** (UIS-PL-52) | Hiding a student's own grades can clash with the student's right to their data under the Data Privacy Act, and with national rules on withholding (for example RA 11984, which bars "no permit, no exam" for unpaid fees). | **Check the policy with CBSUA's legal office and DPO first.** If allowed, prefer holds that block **releasing official documents** (TOR, certifications), not the student's view of their own grades. Every hold has a reason, an office and a log. |
| 8 | **Masterlists with live personal information in Excel** (UIS-PL-36, 46) | Bulk exports of personal data are the easiest leak, and the earlier council found ATLAS has no privacy layer yet. | Allow it through the report runner (theme 3): a separate export permission, only the columns needed, a purpose asked on each export, and every export logged. Mask sensitive fields by default. Have the DPO approve the column catalog. |
| 9 | **Accounts created in advance with passwords handed out** (UIS-PL-13) | Printed default passwords (or ones made from birthdates) are a common way into school systems. | Create accounts in bulk (X-PL-01), but sign people in through **Keycloak**. Where passwords remain, use a one-time temporary password that must be changed at the first sign-in (`security.forcePasswordChange`). |
| 10 | **Dropping or changing statutory content** (UIS-PL-15, 16, 24, 60: logos, header lines, the doc stamp line, the watermark) | These are fair template choices. But a template an administrator can edit freely could also remove what a document needs to be valid. | Template changes need a **registrar sign-off and a preview**, and the template version is kept on every issued document. Some parts cannot be removed: the verification code and QR code, the signatory block, and "Nothing follows" on the TOR. |
