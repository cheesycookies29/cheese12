## 1. The configuration model (Architect, agreed by all)

Not seven separate settings screens. **One shared store of versioned definitions**, which every mechanism uses:
- **The school edits data. The code renders it and enforces the law.**
- **Each signed or posted record keeps the version it used:** its definition id, version and hash, and what it actually printed.
- **Changes are made by one person and approved by another.** An administrator drafts; the owning office approves (the Registrar, the Chief Accountant or the HR Office). An administrator never approves their own change, and is never a step in a business approval route.
- **Effective dates.** Changes that affect the books or official records take effect from a date. Finance changes (numbering, chart of accounts, signatories, templates) need the Chief Accountant's approval. *(Controller)*
- **Every element has a "prescribed" level** *(Guardian)*:
  - **Locked:** changes only with an LTDC release.
  - **Concurrence:** needs the agency's written agreement, for example the COA auditor's, plus a second approver.
  - **School:** the school's own choice.
- **The change log is never trimmed.** Today `server/config.js` keeps only the last 5,000 entries and cannot undo values over 20 kB.

| Mechanism | Items served (about) | What it adds | What stays fixed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Document templates** | 55 | paper size and orientation, versioned letterhead, several logos, signatory slots by role (from a dated `officeHolders` table), footnote tokens ("generated by… on…"), repeated table headers, term or period picker | the QR and verification code; "Nothing follows"; the boxes of COA/DBM/CSC forms; required signature blocks; no free HTML. Reprints come from the snapshot taken at issue |
| **Number series** | 12 | **one** numbering engine (today Finance has its own); a reset period separate from the format; tokens `{F}` `{SF}` `{J}` `{CAMPUS}`; separate BURS and ORS series; the fund passed to every series | an issued number is never reused or edited; gaps are explained; official receipts follow the pre-printed forms |
| **Lookup lists with rules** | 25 | grade remarks (OD, UD, NG, AC…), leave types, appointment and check statuses, journal types, funds under fund clusters, fee types | an internal meaning never changes once used, only its label; the GAM books; the ORS/BURS mapping; CSC leave types can be renamed, not removed |
| **Approval routes** | 22 | steps per document type, OIC/delegates, reminders and escalation | legal steps (for example DV Boxes A–E) cannot be removed or reordered; the requester never approves; **no auto-approval** |
| **Grading policy** | 11 | the grading period, transmutation, rounding and pass marks; activity types teachers can set within limits; rubrics | the board-approved grading system; a section keeps the policy its term opened with; posted grades change only through the grade-change route |
| **Import templates** | 11 | CSV/Excel templates for every master list: accounts, students, employees, sections, enrollments, curricula, opening balances, questions | the same checks as the screens; one transaction per batch; undo of the last batch |
| **Report definitions** | 30 | one period picker (day, month, quarter, year), column choice, .xlsx export, filters across campuses | prescribed columns are locked, and dropping one needs a recorded written waiver; exports with personal data need their own permission and a purpose, and are logged |

**Dependence on the storage move to `node:sqlite`:**
- **Can be built before the move:** the definitions, lookups and office holders are small, so built-ins, checks, printouts and number formatting can be written and tested now.
- **Needs the new store:** version history and logos; number counters, which must commit together with their document (this replaces `finSnap`); approval steps; import batches; report runs.

## 2. Problems already in ATLAS today (found during the review)

These are not new requests. They are defects or risks in the current code, listed for the owner's decision.

| # | Problem | Raised by |
|---|---|---|
| F1 | **Legal pay parameters are plain settings an administrator can change:** GSIS shares, ECC, PERA, overtime rates and cap, the tax-exempt ceiling, the cash gift, PEI and clothing. An administrator can also take tardiness from salary instead of vacation leave first. They should be **dated tables that cite their circular, shipped by LTDC**; an override needs a reason, a second approver and a log entry. | PeopleOps |
| F2 | **Flexible time is never charged undertime.** A 6-hour day is paid as 8 (`shared/calc.js`, `shared/gov-pay.js`), which risks a COA disallowance. **The late grace period** is a free setting, though CSC has none. **A remote day** counts as a full day with no accomplishment report. | PeopleOps |
| F3 | **"SPL" is seeded as Solo Parent Leave.** On the CSC leave form, SPL is Special Privilege Leave. The leave preset also lacks Forced Leave and Wellness Leave, has the private-sector Emergency Leave, and gives credits in full each year instead of accruing them monthly. **The CSC late/undertime conversion** drifts from the table. | PeopleOps, HRIS map |
| F4 | **Finance reprints use today's signatories.** A posted JEV reprinted after a new Chief Accountant takes over shows the new name. Academic already keeps what it issued. | Architect |
| F5 | **There are two numbering engines, and they disagree.** Finance's does not check for numbers already used, and a month in the format forces a monthly reset. This is the cause of FMIS PL-38/40. | Architect, FMIS map |
| F6 | **The ADA Disbursement Journal (GAM App. 52) is missing** from the journals. **Bank reconciliation has App. 81 only,** not App. 80 for the MDS account. **Liquidations post to the General Journal.** | Controller, FMIS map |
| F7 | **The settings change log is capped** (5,000 entries) and cannot undo large values. **The settings import rolls back from a snapshot,** the same pattern as `finSnap`. | Guardian, Architect |
| F8 | **The public check of an issued document** (`/api/verify`) shows the GWA. The DPO should decide whether it should. | Guardian |
| F9 | **There is no Daily Time Record printout** (CS Form 48), which COA requires. | PeopleOps |

## 3. Small things users can't find, or that already exist

*(Advocate, maps)*
- The registrar's name is already a setting (UIS-PL-20).
- **Print** always uses the current term, though the server accepts another. This one cause is behind four certification requests (UIS-PL-32/34/43/45).
- Pop-up notifications exist (HRIS PL-065).
- Finance list pages wait for a **Show** or **Find** button (FMIS PL-58). They should load on open, as one rule for the whole UI.
- Dashboards exist. Keep "Waiting for you" at the top, with preset cards per role, not a dashboard builder.

## 6. Working method with the offices (FMIS PL-116)

- **People:**
  - one **focal person per CBSUA office**, with a named counterpart at LTDC per module;
  - **60–90 minute walkthroughs** of one real transaction in each office: Registrar, HR, Accounting, Budget, Cashier, Supply/BAC and a faculty group.
- **A form register.** Each form has a status, an owner office, its agency and citation, and a prescribed level.
- **Configuration screens.** Office staff, not developers, should be able to use them safely:
  - **draft → preview with a real record at real paper size → test → publish**;
  - locked parts shown with a padlock and the reason.
- **Sign-off and testing:**
  - sign-off by the head who signs the form;
  - the DPO co-signs anything with personal data;
  - the COA auditor's agreement is attached for concurrence-level changes;
  - testing replays last month's real cases on a training copy, with synthetic or masked data.
- **The configuration of record.** Each sign-off saves a configuration export (versioned, with effective dates). CBSUA keeps it.
- **Declined requests** are answered in writing, with a demo of the alternative, and CBSUA acknowledges them.

## Verdict

**The council recommends that ATLAS meet these requests mainly through configuration, on one shared,
versioned and guarded configuration store, after the storage and AWS gates, not before them.** About a
quarter of the list needs no build. About 70 items are new modules for later, separately priced waves.
Six requests are declined with alternatives.

The first two weeks need no code, only CBSUA's samples and decisions. The defects in section 2 should be
fixed early, because they are wrong today whatever CBSUA asks.

**Awaiting the owner's approval:** which steps to start, and in particular:
- whether to fix the defects in section 2;
- whether to send the answer sheet and requests pack to CBSUA.
