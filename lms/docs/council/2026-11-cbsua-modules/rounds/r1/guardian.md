## Guardian Position — CBSUA deployment of Lancaster ATLAS (Round 1)

Severity labels: **Mandatory** (DPA/NPC/COA exposure) · **Strongly Recommended** · **Advisory**.
Roles assumed: CBSUA is the **Personal Information Controller (PIC)**; LTDC is a **Personal Information Processor (PIP)**; AWS, Cloudflare, DuckDNS, Let's Encrypt and GitHub are sub-processors or service providers in the chain.

### 1. Opening perspective

ATLAS already has good security controls: scrypt hashes encrypted with a separate key, a hash-chained append-only audit log, an encrypted EBS volume, geolocation retention with purging, and masked data on the public verify page. It has almost no **privacy governance layer** on top of them. No screen shows a privacy notice. There is no workflow for data subject rights, no retention schedule beyond geolocation and monitor IPs, and nothing records who *read* sensitive records. Every module except Facilities holds personal data, and Academic and People also hold **sensitive personal information** under RA 10173 Sec. 3(l): religion, government-issued ID numbers, health documents and precise location. For a state university, those gaps are compliance gaps, not polish. The most important question from my lens is this: **before go-live, has CBSUA (the PIC) signed off on where this data lives, who processes it and for how long, through a PIA, an outsourcing agreement with LTDC that covers AWS Singapore and Cloudflare, and a retention schedule aligned with the NAP GRDS?** Without that, every module below inherits the same exposure.

### 2. Module input

**ATLAS home (/).** It holds identity, sign-in metadata, profile pictures and announcements. `docs/account-profile.md:54` already flags that a student directory would expose minors' details, and it correctly limits other people's profiles to admins. ATLAS keeps only some IPs short: security-monitor addresses are kept 7 days (`docs/security-monitor.md:60-64`), but the audit trail also records IP and user agent on every sign-in and change (`server/api.js:188,241,827`), with no expiry. *Recommendation (Mandatory):* put a link to a **privacy notice** (DPA Sec. 16(b), 18) on the sign-in page and in My profile. Add a "My data" page where users can request access, correction or erasure, routed to the DPO, with a tracked response deadline.

**Learn + Admin panel.** Public self-registration for applicants (`server/api.js:297-320`, `settings.registration.open`) collects personal data with no notice and no consent step. A grep of `public/js` for "privacy" or "consent" finds nothing. Guardian links let parents see grades, attendance and schedule (`server/guardian.js:1-7`) with no age check and no student consent, yet most CBSUA students are adults and are the data subjects of their own grades. *Recommendation (Mandatory):* show a privacy notice on registration. Change guardian links so that, for students aged 18 or over, the link needs the **student's recorded consent**, and links for students under 18 are reviewed when the student turns 18. Record when and by whom each link was made.

**Academic (UIS).** It holds the most sensitive data. `server/academic.js:478` stores `religion`, `sex`, `civilStatus`, `address` and `guardianContact`. The requirements checklist covers the PSA birth certificate, a **medical/dental certificate** and a police clearance (`shared/academic.js:88`), which are health and criminal-record-adjacent data (sensitive PI). The public `/api/verify/:code` route returns the name, the GWA and units (`server/academic.js:811-821`): that disclosure is proportionate only if the printed document says so. Student permanent records (TOR, grades) must be kept **permanently** under the NAP GRDS for SUCs and CHED rules. FHE billing (RA 10931) sends student data to CHED and UniFAST, which is lawful under the statute, but it should be logged as a disclosure. *Recommendation (Mandatory):* **drop religion** unless CBSUA names a legal basis. Classify uploaded requirement documents as sensitive, with restricted roles and a disposal date once they have been checked. Log every FHE/CHED export as a data-sharing event.

**People (HRIS).** Employee records hold `tin`, `sssNo`, `philhealthNo`, `pagibigNo`, `bankName` and `bankAccount` (`server/hris.js:55`), which are government-issued IDs and therefore sensitive PI under Sec. 3(l)(3). `employeeView` returns them unmasked in list and detail responses (`server/hris.js:330,434-435`). Clock-in stores latitude and longitude to six decimals, about 10 cm (`server/hris-geo.js:30`), kept 365 days by default and configurable up to 3,650 (`server/hris-geo.js:90`, `shared/geo.js:6`). There is a notice (`public/js/hris/geo.js:22`), but it gives neither the purpose nor the retention period. *Recommendation (Strongly Recommended):* **mask government IDs and bank numbers** in lists and reveal them only to payroll roles when needed. Store only `inside` and `distance` for clock-ins, not raw coordinates. Lower the default to 90 days and cap it lower, and expand the notice to give the purpose and retention period.

**Finance (FMIS).** The card file and payees hold TINs and bank accounts (`server/finance-cash.js:31-35,212,289-303`). These are COA-governed records: disbursement vouchers, ORs and RCDs must stay unaltered and available for COA post-audit, and disposal follows the NAP GRDS and COA rules, not ATLAS defaults. Finance's own `finAudit` and the rollback snapshot on failed writes (`server/api.js:807,815`) are good. *Recommendation (Mandatory):* write a **records retention and disposal matrix** per Finance document type (DV, ORS, OR, RCD, payroll, IIRUP), signed off by CBSUA's records officer and COA auditor, and block deletion of any record still inside its retention period. Add these to the "items to confirm" in `docs/FINANCE-PLAN.md` §5.

**System (Users & Access, Audit log, Security monitor).** The audit log is hash-chained and append-only (`server/audit.js:1-12`), but only **non-GET** requests by staff are recorded (`server/api.js:822`). Reading a student record or an employee's IDs leaves no trace. The full-database download `GET /api/admin/backup` (`server/api.js:452-455`) sends every record ATLAS holds to an admin's laptop. It only calls `monitor.noteBackup`, which keeps the time and name of the latest download (`server/monitor.js:407`), so no audit-log entry is ever written for it. The audit log itself has no retention period. *Recommendation (Mandatory):* audit **reads of sensitive records** (student profile, employee IDs, document payloads) and **every backup download**. Require a second admin or DPO approval for downloads, or encrypt the downloaded file, consistent with NPC Circular 16-01's limits on copying personal data from government systems. Set a retention period for the audit log, for example 5 years, with archiving.

**Facilities (planned).** No code exists yet (0 routes). It is mostly non-personal data (rooms, assets), but bookings carry names and contacts, and maintenance tickets may carry staff data. *Recommendation (Advisory):* when it is built, apply privacy by design. Store only the booker's account ID, purge booking history after a set period, and reuse Finance's property register (`docs/FINANCE-PLAN.md`) instead of keeping a second copy of asset custodian data.

**REX e-Reader (planned).** At present it is only a card (`shared/systems.js:60-72`) for students, teachers, registrars, deans and admins. Once connected, ATLAS will send student identities, and probably enrolment and class membership, to **REX Bookstore, a private commercial third party**. That is a data sharing arrangement under NPC Circular 2020-03, and reading activity is behavioural data. *Recommendation (Mandatory before switch-on):* sign a **Data Sharing Agreement** between CBSUA and REX that sets purpose limitation, a **ban on marketing use and resale**, where the data is stored, breach notification and deletion at the end of term. Use **pseudonymous SSO** (an opaque ID, no name or student number unless needed). Default to opt-in for students, and do not allow any analytics pixel from REX inside ATLAS.

### 3. Top 3 concerns (ranked)

1. **No privacy governance layer, while sensitive PI is already being collected (Mandatory).** *Evidence:* there is no privacy notice or consent text anywhere in `public/js`, including applicant self-registration (`server/api.js:297-320`). Sensitive PI is held: religion (`server/academic.js:478`), government IDs and bank data (`server/hris.js:55`, `server/finance-cash.js:34`), medical certificates (`shared/academic.js:88`) and precise geolocation (`server/hris-geo.js:30`). There is no data subject rights workflow. Guardians see adult students' grades without the student's consent (`server/guardian.js`). *Required:* CBSUA's DPO is designated and registered with the NPC (NPC Circular 2022-04; government agencies must register), a PIA covers each module, privacy notices are in place, a rights-request workflow exists, guardian consent is fixed, and religion is removed or justified.

2. **Processor chain, data residency and change control are not documented or controlled by the PIC (Mandatory / Strongly Recommended).** *Evidence:* production runs on AWS Singapore (`deploy/aws/EC2.md:14,54`), optionally behind Cloudflare, which terminates TLS and so sees all traffic in clear text (`deploy/aws/EC2.md:152`). The address is a DuckDNS name rather than a gov.ph or edu.ph domain. Code **auto-deploys every 10 minutes from a development branch** (`deploy/aws/EC2.md:91`), so the vendor can change how data for tens of thousands of people is processed without CBSUA approving it. The DPA allows transfers abroad, but under Sec. 21 the PIC stays accountable. *Required:* an outsourcing and subcontracting agreement between CBSUA and LTDC (IRR Secs. 43-45) that names AWS and Cloudflare as sub-processors, a check of compliance with the DICT Cloud First Policy and data classification, an official domain, and a **release gate approved by CBSUA** (a tagged release branch and a change log) in place of auto-deploy from a working branch. The on-premise option remains available if the PIA finds the Singapore residency unacceptable.

3. **Retention, erasure and audit of access are incomplete (Mandatory).** *Evidence:* the only retention rules in code cover geolocation (`server/hris-geo.js:44-57`) and monitor IPs (7 days). Account deletion is a hard splice (`server/admin.js:237-252`), but nightly copies keep it for 30 days and snapshots for 7. Uploaded files are mirrored with `rsync -a` **without `--delete`** (`deploy/aws/ltdc-lms-backup.cron`), so documents deleted in ATLAS stay in `backups/files/` with no end date. Reads and full backup downloads are not in the audit trail (`server/api.js:452,822`). There is no retention matrix for permanent academic records or COA records. *Required:* a NAP GRDS-aligned retention matrix per record type (permanent: academic records; COA-governed: Finance and payroll; short: geolocation, sessions, uploaded requirements), deletion that also reaches backups and file mirrors once data is past retention, and audit entries for sensitive reads and exports, so that CBSUA can answer an NPC "who accessed this and when" request within the 72-hour breach-notification window (NPC Circular 16-03).

**Dependencies on other domains:**
- Architect: a read-audit hook in the router and per-field masking.
- Operator: an encrypted, offsite backup that honours deletion, and a release gate.
- Advocate: privacy notice and consent UX.
- CBSUA: DPO sign-off, the records officer's retention schedule, and the REX DSA negotiation.
