A: Backups exist for disaster recovery, not to keep records: permanent academic records and COA records are kept and disposed of in the live database under the NAP GRDS matrix, so every immutable copy expires on a fixed short clock (S3 Object Lock in Compliance mode, 35 days, plus a lifecycle rule that deletes it), and anything erased or disposed of in the live data is gone from all backups within 35 days, with no crypto-shredding and no DPA exemption needed. So that a restore never brings erased data back, ATLAS keeps an erasure ledger (opaque subject ids only) that the restore runbook replays before the restored copy takes traffic, and a quarterly restore drill passes only if no erased id survives. I would add no second foreign region until the PIA clears one: the off-site copy goes to a separate, locked-down AWS account in the same Singapore region (ap-southeast-1), which covers account compromise, deletion and ransomware without a new residency, and the geographic copy is an encrypted nightly pull to storage CBSUA owns in the Philippines, under the same 35-day expiry.
