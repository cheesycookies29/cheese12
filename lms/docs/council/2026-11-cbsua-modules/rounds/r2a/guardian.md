A: Use retention-expiry plus a documented legal-retention position, and do not use crypto-shredding: per-subject keys in a whole-file db.json are heavy machinery we cannot verify by W1. DPA Sec. 16(e) erasure does not override retention the law requires (COA, NAP GRDS, CHED permanent records), so those records are exempt in a signed retention matrix. For everything else, Object Lock retention must equal the backup rotation (for example 35 days, in governance mode, not compliance mode), and an "erasure ledger" of ids is re-applied after any restore so erased people do not come back. I read `server/audit.js`, and the log does **not** store only opaque ids: each entry keeps `actor` and `target` names in clear, `ip`, `agent`, and field `changes` with `from`/`to` values of up to 200 characters (for example `server/system.js:206` writes name, username and email when an account is made). Fix it going forward: keep only `actorId`/`targetId` in the hashed entry, resolve names at display time from a side table that erasure can blank, record only field names (not values) for sensitive fields, and give the log a 5-year retention with sealed, archivable segments so old segments can be deleted without breaking the chain of the live segment.
